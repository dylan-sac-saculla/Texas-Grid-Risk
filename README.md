# The Texas Grid Squeeze

An interactive web page about climate risk for an electric grid operator in Texas (ERCOT). Hot summer afternoons drive record air-conditioning demand, and hard freezes push heating demand up while freezing gas supply and power plants offline. That is a **correlated supply-and-demand shock**: the same weather moves both sides of the balance in the wrong direction.

Four sliders control the outdoor temperature (hazard), how well plants and gas supply are winterized (vulnerability), demand growth (exposure), and new firm capacity and batteries (adaptation). A live chart shows peak-hour demand and available supply across temperatures, with the shortfall shaded red, and a table reports the margin and the approximate number of homes that would lose power. Preset buttons recreate Winter Storm Uri (Feb 2021), the January 2024 cold snap, and the August 2023 heat record.

Numbers are calibrated to ERCOT's recorded peaks and to Winter Storm Uri; sources are listed on the page.
